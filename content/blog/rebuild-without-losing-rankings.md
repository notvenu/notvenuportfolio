A redesign is the most common way a business loses its search traffic. The new site looks better, launches, and a few weeks later the enquiries dry up. Nothing was "penalised". The new site simply threw away the signals the old one had built.

When I moved [3D Auto Artist](https://3dautoartist.com) off Elementor onto a hand-built WordPress theme, the rule was simple: **change how it's built, not what Google sees.** Here's the checklist.

## Before you touch anything: take an inventory

1. **Crawl the live site.** Export every URL, with its title, meta description, canonical and H1.
2. **Pull the pages that matter** from Google Search Console: which URLs get impressions and clicks, and for which queries.
3. **Save it.** This list is the contract the new site has to honour.

## Keep the addresses

Every URL that exists today should either exist on the new site at **the same path**, or **301-redirect** to its closest replacement. Not to the home page: to the page that answers the same question.

## Carry the words over

The page title, meta description and main heading are a big part of why a page ranks for what it ranks for. On the 3D Auto Artist rebuild, titles, meta and canonicals were carried over **word for word**. Improve them later, one at a time, where you can measure the effect, not all at once on launch day.

## Don't lose content in the redesign

Designers love trimming copy. But a paragraph that answers "how long does ceramic coating last?" may be exactly what that page ranks for. Keep the substance, even if the layout changes around it.

## Keep the technical basics

- Same `robots.txt` rules, and a fresh **XML sitemap** submitted in Search Console on launch day.
- Structured data (LocalBusiness, FAQ, Product) carried over or improved.
- No accidental `noindex` left over from the staging site. This one is more common than it should be.
- Faster, not slower. A rebuild is the moment to fix image sizes and drop unused scripts.

## After launch: watch, don't guess

For the first few weeks, check Search Console for **404s and coverage errors**, and compare impressions for the pages from your inventory. A dip for a few days is normal while Google re-crawls. A page that falls away and stays down means something on the list was missed.

A rebuild done this way keeps everything the old site earned and adds the speed and polish of the new one.
