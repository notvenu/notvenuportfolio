People don't only "Google it" any more. The same question, *who does car PPF near me?*, gets typed into Google, spoken to a phone, and asked to ChatGPT. Each of those surfaces picks its answer differently, so there are three jobs, not one.

## SEO: ranking in the results

**Search engine optimisation** is the classic job: getting a page into the list of blue links, as high as possible.

What actually moves it for a small business site:

- **One clear page per thing you sell.** A page for "ceramic coating" and another for "paint protection film" beats one page that mentions both.
- **Titles and meta descriptions written for the search.** The title is the blue link. It should say what you do and where, in the words customers use.
- **Canonicals and redirects.** Every page should have one address. Old URLs should redirect to their new home instead of returning a 404.
- **Speed on a cheap phone.** Most local searches happen on mobile data. Heavy page builders and uncompressed images cost rankings and customers.

## AEO: being the answer

**Answer engine optimisation** is about the boxes that answer a question *without* a click: featured snippets, "People also ask", voice assistants.

These pull a short, self-contained answer from a page. So:

- **Ask the question in a heading, answer it in the first sentence below.** "How long does PPF last?" followed by a direct answer, then the detail.
- **Use FAQ and HowTo structured data** where the content really is a FAQ or a how-to. It tells the engine exactly which text is the answer.
- **Keep answers in the HTML.** Content hidden behind a click-to-load script may never be read.

## GEO: getting cited by AI

**Generative engine optimisation** is the newest: making a site easy for ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity to read, trust and cite when they write an answer.

AI assistants summarise many sources, so they favour pages that are easy to extract facts from:

- **State the facts plainly.** Who you are, what you sell, where you work, prices or ranges if you can share them. Marketing fluff gives a model nothing to quote.
- **Be consistent everywhere.** The same name, address and services on your site, Google Business Profile and directories.
- **Publish an `llms.txt`.** A plain-text summary of the site at `/llms.txt`, the AI-era cousin of `robots.txt`.
- **Don't block the crawlers** you want to be cited by. Check `robots.txt` isn't shutting out the assistants.

## The overlap

Most of the work serves all three. A fast page with one clear topic, honest headings, direct answers and structured data ranks better, gets pulled into answer boxes more, and is easier for an AI to cite. That's why I treat them as one job: build the site right, then keep checking it shows up wherever people are asking.
